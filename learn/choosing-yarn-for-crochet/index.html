How to choose yarn for crochet

Learn how yarn fiber, weight, texture, and care instructions affect your crochet project, from amigurumi to home decor.

Start with the project, then choose the yarn

A coaster, a soft toy, and a drapey scarf need different things from their yarn. Before shopping, check the pattern’s recommended fiber, yarn weight, hook size, and yardage. If you substitute a yarn, match more than its color: thickness and fiber change how the finished piece feels and behaves.

Cotton, acrylic, and wool: what changes?

Cotton has clear stitch definition and relatively little stretch, making it useful for many home accessories. Acrylic can be an approachable option for practice and soft decorative projects; check its care label. Wool is elastic and warm, but its washing requirements vary. No fiber is right for every project. For anything exposed to heat, follow the pattern’s material requirements and the yarn manufacturer’s guidance.

Understand yarn weight

Yarn weight describes thickness, not the weight of the ball. Names such as DK, worsted, and bulky help you compare options, but two yarns in the same category can still differ. Compare the recommended gauge and the length per ball. Keep the ball band so you can check fiber content and dye lot later.

Choose a yarn that makes stitches visible

For your first crochet projects, a smooth, light-colored yarn makes it easier to see the V-shaped tops of stitches. Very fluffy, dark, or highly textured yarn can hide the loops you need to work into. Try a small practice square before committing to a large project.

Swatch before substituting

Make a swatch with the suggested hook and compare it with the pattern’s gauge instructions. Measure without stretching it; wash and dry it as directed if the final item will be washed. A larger or smaller hook changes stitch size, while fiber can change drape even when gauge matches.

Plan the amount and care

Estimate yardage from the pattern, rather than assuming every ball contains the same length. Buy enough in the same dye lot when consistent color matters. Save the washing instructions and include them with a handmade gift.

Reference standards

For terminology and yarn labels, consult the Craft Yarn Council crochet abbreviations and yarn weight guidelines.
